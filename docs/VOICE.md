# Voice

The words in ForgeX are part of the product. A founder should feel one careful writer behind every screen: the hero line, a button, an error at midnight, the alt text a screen reader speaks. This file is how that writer works.

## Who reads this

**Founders.**
- First-year students at Mesa School of Business, aged 20 to 23. Most studied commerce, and many grew up around a family business.
- They are curious and a little nervous, and they read on their phones, often between other things.
- Few have called themselves founders or engineers before.
- Every line should leave them feeling more capable, and certain what to do next.

**The Mesa team**, in the console.
- They are reviewing 117 people at speed.
- Their copy is plain, short and functional. No warmth is needed there, only clarity.

## How we sound

**We talk to one person.** "You", in the present tense, active voice. Not "students", not "users", not "we're excited to".

**We treat them as founders.** No talking down, no cheerleading, no customer-service politeness. A founder is someone with real work to do, and we help them do it.

**Warm, never cute.** Kindness shows in clarity and in respect for their time. It doesn't show in puns, "oops" or emoji.

**Confident, never loud.** We state things. We don't promise transformations.

**Specific over general.** A number, a place or a real moment does more work than any adjective.
- "Kiranas lose a sale every time a shelf runs empty" beats "inefficiencies in retail".
- "Tell us where to look" beats "personalise your experience".

**Once is enough.** If a word can go without losing meaning, it goes. If a heading repeats what's already on screen, the heading goes.

**What we learn from:**
- Stripe's docs, for exactness.
- Linear, for restraint.
- GOV.UK, for plain words and respect for the reader in a hurry.

We learn from them; we don't copy them.

## The rules, string by string

### Headings
- Say what matters on this screen, ideally in eight words or fewer.
- Never label what's already visible. A form doesn't need a heading that says "Form".
- A question is fine when the screen is a question: "Who can you reach this week?"

### Buttons
- A verb that names the outcome, under 24 characters: "I'll build this", "Show my matches", "Send my why".
- Never "Submit", "OK", "Click here", "Continue" alone, or "Yes".
- First person ("I'll build this") is right when the founder is committing to something. Otherwise use the imperative ("Download card").

### Body
- One idea per sentence.
- Sentences under 20 words; paragraphs under three sentences.
- Lead with what the reader needs, not with what we did.

### Errors
- Say what happened, then what to do. Never blame.
- If their input is safe, tell them, because that's the first thing they fear.
- Good: "That didn't save. Your answers are still here. Try again in a moment."
- "Something went wrong" never stands alone. It always comes with what to do.

### Empty and waiting states
- Give a reason and a next step, or say nothing at all.
- "You'll see our response here." works because the reason is obvious and the next step is to wait.

### Labels and placeholders
- A label is what someone would say out loud: "Your city", not "City field".
- A placeholder shows an example, never an instruction:
  - "Pune", not "Enter your city".
  - "github.com/ananya", not "Paste your GitHub link".
- A placeholder never holds information the founder needs, because it disappears once they type.

### Nudges and helper text
- Ask the next question rather than correcting: "Picture one person. Who are they?"
- One nudge at a time.

### Accessibility text
- Alt text describes what the image adds: "Cartographer, holding a compass and a rolled map". Never "image of".
- Decorative images get an empty alt.
- ARIA labels read like speech: "Aarav, Scout", not "founder-card-aarav".

### Page titles, meta, emails, toasts
These are copy too. Page titles say where you are: "Your why · ForgeX". An email subject says what happened and to which thing.

## Words

**Use, because they are concrete and true:**
build, make, fix, find, talk to, reach, pay, cost, save, week, shop, owner, customer, problem, real, first, next, three weeks, your, today, this week

**Avoid, with what to say instead:**

| Avoid | Say instead |
| --- | --- |
| revolutionise, transform | change, fix |
| seamless, frictionless | say what is easy: "in one tap" |
| leverage | use |
| unlock, empower, elevate, supercharge | say what they can now do |
| synergy, ecosystem | say who works with whom |
| cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, game-changer | name the thing |
| robust, scalable (to founders) | say what holds up, and under what |
| delve, let's dive in | start |
| journey (about the user) | the six steps; your week; the sprint |
| in today's world, nowadays | cut it |
| oops, uh-oh | say what happened |
| something went wrong (alone) | say what happened and what to do |
| user (to founders) | the person, the owner, the customer |
| solution (in a problem statement) | cut it; that's the founder's work |
| simply, just, easily | cut it; it may not be simple for them |
| please (in buttons) | cut it |
| amazing, awesome, exciting, incredible | cut it; show why it matters |

## Problem statements

Each one gets the care of the hero line, because it's the line a founder may live with for three weeks.

- **Title.** Under ten words, concrete, a little provocative. Name the moment of pain, not the category.
  - Good: "The invoice that quietly costs more every month".
  - Bad: "Invoice management for SMEs".
- **Problem.** Two or three sentences:
  - what's broken;
  - how often it happens;
  - what it costs;
  - and one specific detail from the evidence.

  It names no target user, no solution and no tool that hints at one.
- **Challenge.** One line. It starts with a verb and sets the bar ("within the hour", "in a day", "without a phone call") without naming a product.
- **The test.** Read it once. Would a 19-year-old want to spend three weeks on it? If not, it's either not specific enough or not true enough.

**Before (P040)**
> When the bus does not come, nobody finds another
>
> Booked intercity buses get cancelled a day before or never reach the boarding point, and passengers are not told. The booking service offers no alternate bus and no status on a refund.
>
> When a bus is cancelled, get the passenger onto another one before they reach the stop.

**After**
> The bus that never arrives, and nobody tells you
>
> Intercity buses are cancelled the day before travel, or never reach the boarding point at all, and no one warns the passenger. They find out at the roadside, with no other bus offered and no word on their refund. The same story turns up in reports from December 2025, June 2026 and October 2026.
>
> Get a stranded passenger onto the next bus before they've left the stop.

Why the "after" is better:
- **Title:** it names the moment, standing at the roadside.
- **Problem:** it shows the cost, and the dates show that it keeps happening.
- **Challenge:** it starts with a verb and sets a bar you could test on a Saturday.
- **Every detail is in the evidence.** A rewrite can sharpen a problem, but it can never add a fact the evidence doesn't hold.

## Lines the owner wrote

These stay exactly as written unless a proposed replacement is clearly better. In that case both are shown with the reason, and the owner chooses:

- This is you so far. Change anything that doesn't feel like you.
- I'll build this
- None of these? Write your own.
- Tell us why you want to build this. We'll tell you if it's the right fit for you.
- You'll see our response here.
- Portfolio or personal site
- Other / Somewhere else

## How this is enforced

- **One source.** Every string lives in `content/copy.ts`. Strings in components are a bug.
- **Copy lint.** A Vitest suite reads the copy and fails the build on:
  - banned words;
  - buttons of 24 characters or more;
  - headings over eight words;
  - exclamation marks outside the reveal and the card;
  - decorative quote marks;
  - images without alt text.
- **Bank lint.** `pnpm bank:lint` checks every problem statement:
  - length limits;
  - banned words;
  - solution words in the problem field;
  - a challenge that doesn't start with a verb.

  Every flagged item is fixed by hand.
- **In context.** Every screen is read aloud at 360px and 1440px before it ships. Nothing truncates or breaks a line in the wrong place, and a new student knows what to do within three seconds.
